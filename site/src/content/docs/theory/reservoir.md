## Overview

The Liquid State Machine (LSM) reservoir is the core computational component of the Video Denoise system.

Its purpose is to transform incoming audio spike trains into rich, high-dimensional temporal representations that can be decoded into an improved audio signal.

Unlike conventional neural networks, the reservoir itself is not trained using backpropagation.

Instead, computation emerges through the interaction of:

- Recurrent connectivity
- Spiking dynamics
- Synaptic delays
- Excitatory and inhibitory populations
- Temporal integration

The reservoir acts as a dynamic processing medium, often referred to as the *liquid*.

---

## Why A Reservoir?

Audio denoising is fundamentally a temporal problem.

A sound occurring now is often related to sounds that occurred milliseconds earlier.

Traditional feedforward neural networks struggle to retain this temporal context without additional memory mechanisms.

Liquid State Machines solve this problem by exploiting the natural dynamics of recurrent spiking networks.

Incoming spikes create patterns of activity that persist for a short period of time, allowing the network to maintain a fading memory of previous events.

This temporal memory forms the basis of signal reconstruction and denoising.

## High-Level Architecture

The reservoir receives spike trains generated by the RMS encoder.

```text
Audio
  │
  ▼
RMS Encoder
  │
  ▼
Input Neurons
  │
  ▼
LSM Reservoir
  │
  ▼
Readout Layer
  │
  ▼
Reconstructed Audio
```

The role of the reservoir is to convert relatively simple input spike patterns into complex internal states that contain useful information about the audio signal.

## Connectivity Strategy

The reservoir is intentionally sparse.

Rather than connecting every neuron to every other neuron, neurons only form a limited number of connections.

Initial target:

```text
Connection Probability = 10%
```

This provides:

- Reduced computational complexity
- Increased diversity of activity
- Improved stability
- Greater biological plausibility

---

## Connectome-Inspired Design

The reservoir is inspired by principles observed in biological connectomes.

Rather than replicating a specific nervous system, the design borrows several architectural concepts:

- Sparse connectivity
- Local clustering
- Recurrent loops
- Small-world topology
- Distributed computation

These characteristics encourage rich internal dynamics while limiting excessive activity.

---

## Small-World Networks

Connectivity will be generated using a small-world topology.

Small-world networks provide:

- Short average path lengths
- High clustering
- Efficient information propagation

Conceptually:

```text
Random Network
      ↓
Add Local Structure
      ↓
Small-World Reservoir
```

This approach provides a balance between order and randomness.

---

## Excitatory Connections

Excitatory neurons increase the probability that connected neurons will fire.

Their role is to:

- Propagate information
- Maintain reservoir activity
- Distribute temporal patterns

Without excitation, information cannot effectively spread through the network.

---

## Inhibitory Connections

Inhibitory neurons suppress downstream activity.

Their role is equally important.

Benefits include:

- Network stability
- Sparse coding
- Noise suppression
- Competition between neurons

Without inhibition, the reservoir risks becoming saturated with activity.

---

## Lateral Inhibition

The reservoir incorporates lateral inhibition mechanisms.

When a neuron becomes active:

```text
Neuron Fires
      │
      ▼
Local Inhibitory Response
      │
      ▼
Neighbour Suppression
```

This creates competition between neurons and encourages selective responses.

Expected benefits:

- Reduced redundancy
- Improved signal discrimination
- More informative reservoir states

---

## Synaptic Delays

Biological signalling is not instantaneous.

To capture this behaviour, connections include transmission delays.

Initial delay range:

```text
1ms - 15ms
```

The delay assigned to each connection is selected randomly within this range.

The result is a richer set of temporal dynamics.

Different components of the network effectively operate on different time scales.

---

## Synapse Models

Two reservoir configurations will be investigated.

### Static Synapses

Weights remain fixed throughout the simulation.

Benefits:

- Simplicity
- Reproducibility
- Useful baseline

---

### Plastic Synapses

Weights adapt according to neural activity.

Benefits:

- Learning capability
- Adaptation
- Biological realism

Plasticity is explored through STDP.

---

## Reservoir State

The reservoir's internal state can be described using several measures:

- Spike counts
- Firing rates
- Filtered spike traces
- Membrane potentials

These representations form the input to the readout layer.

The readout layer does not observe the original audio.

It only observes the current state of the reservoir.

---

## Why Reservoir Computing?

A key advantage of reservoir computing is that the recurrent network itself requires relatively little optimisation.

Training is concentrated in the readout layer.

This allows:

- Faster experimentation
- Reduced training complexity
- Greater interpretability
- Easier comparison of biological mechanisms

The reservoir therefore functions as a reusable computational substrate.

## Working Hypothesis

The central hypothesis for the reservoir is:

> A sparse recurrent reservoir incorporating excitatory and inhibitory populations, synaptic delays, and biologically-inspired connectivity will generate richer temporal representations than a static feedforward architecture and improve audio reconstruction performance.

The validity of this hypothesis will be evaluated using quantitative audio metrics and spike-train similarity measures.
